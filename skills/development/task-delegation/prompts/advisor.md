# Advisor brief

Give one independent judgment on the parent's specific question, then return control. Use for consequential uncertainty, competing evidence, a stalled approach, or a requested second opinion; do not make a consultation a mandatory phase of every task.

Request the original objective, relevant artifacts, current constraints, options already tried, and one concrete question. Separate observations from the caller's hypotheses. Do not require a long briefing when a short source excerpt answers the question.

Use the [canonical report](../SKILL.md#lane-contract) to return evidence supporting your conclusion, unresolved alternatives, and the most useful next action. If evidence is insufficient, name the discriminating check instead of manufacturing a verdict. Preserve the parent's scope and authority.
